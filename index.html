About me

PhD candidate in time-domain astrophysics
Einstein Probe Science Center · Beijing, China

I’m a PhD candidate at the National Astronomical Observatories, Chinese Academy of Sciences, where I work with Weimin Yuan at the Einstein Probe Science Center.

I study what happens when stars explode and compact objects merge. I use observations from Einstein Probe and wide-field optical surveys to look for the optical counterparts of fast X-ray transients. Finding that counterpart helps connect a brief X-ray signal to its source.

During my master’s at the University of Science and Technology of China, I developed a light-curve classifier to pick out kilonova candidates in simulated observations from the Wide Field Survey Telescope.

Outside my research, I enjoy tennis, cycling, basketball, and travel. I also like experimenting with AI agents.

    The Astrophysical Journal 999, 239 (2026)

    DOI arXiv
    Abstract

    Extragalactic fast X-ray transients (eFXTs) represent a rapidly growing class of high-energy phenomena, whose physical origins remain poorly understood. With its wide-field, sensitive all-sky monitoring, the Einstein Probe (EP) has greatly increased the discovery rate of eFXTs. The search for and identification of the optical counterparts of eFXTs are vital for understanding their classification and constraining their physical origin. Yet, a considerable fraction of eFXTs still lack secure classifications due to the absence of timely follow-up observations. We carry out a systematic search of publicly available optical survey data and transient databases (including the Zwicky Transient Facility and the Transient Name Server) for optical counterparts to eFXT candidates detected by EP. In this paper, we describe our ongoing program and report the first results. Specifically, we identified the eFXT EP240506a to be associated with a UV/optical counterpart, AT 2024ofs. Spectroscopy of its host galaxy with the Very Large Telescope yields a redshift of z = 0.120 ± 0.002. By combining archival survey data with early-time multiwavelength observations, we find that the luminosity and light-curve evolution of AT 2024ofs are consistent with a core-collapse supernova origin. From detectability simulations, we estimate a local event rate density of 8.8 (+21.2, −3.9) events per cubic gigaparsec per year for EP240506a-like events, and completeness-corrected rate of about 36–78 yr−1 Gpc−3 for EP-detected X-ray transients associated with supernovae. Our results demonstrate the potential of EP to uncover prompt high-energy emission from core-collapse supernovae and underscore the critical importance of timely follow-up of future eFXT events.

Projects

  • KN Classifier

    A temporal convolutional network for identifying kilonova candidates from light curves, tested on simulated observations from the Wide Field Survey Telescope. Read the paper

  • EPTools

    A Python toolkit for extracting X-ray light curves and fitting spectra from Einstein Probe and Swift-XRT observations.

  • Small Rotation

    A Python backtest of a 20-day momentum strategy that rotates between the CSI 100, the ChiNext Index, and cash.

  • GCN Parser

    A language-model tool that extracts coordinates, redshifts, and photometry from NASA GCN Circulars and assembles light-curve tables.
